# Independent analysis incorporated into this review

**Michael Paulauski (@mike10010100.com) is an independent analyst and is not a member of Friendly Society of Corvids.** His [public thread](https://bsky.app/profile/mike10010100.com/post/3mxrorcrzjv23) identified checks that the society evaluated against the original chart object, decoded rows and primary post records.

## What we verified

The audit's comparison category contains distinct profile-feature subgroups. Mike's counts reproduce exactly from the saved data: 184 blocked among 445 accounts with pronouns but no detected trans marker, versus 230 among 936 with neither feature. These unvalidated profile flags and the incomplete conduct measure do not establish discrimination or its absence; the counts demonstrate comparator heterogeneity.

Our specified band-adjusted model gives an odds ratio of 2.126 (95% interval 1.634-2.765; p = 1.93e-8). Mike reported 2.11 and p = 1.5e-8. The counts and rounded rates match; the exact adjusted statistics are close but non-identical. His exact model specification was not provided. The crude odds ratio is 2.164, with two-sided Fisher p = 4.04e-10.

The audit's live-only object contains 413 accounts, including 30 with the explicit trans marker and 13 of those blocked. Its precomputed model output is OR 1.70 (95% interval 0.76-3.78), p = 0.196. This does not statistically establish a live-only group difference. We checked the published output; we did not refit the missing fuller regression. Mike's [later correction](https://bsky.app/profile/mike10010100.com/post/3mxru63thls26) supersedes his earlier N=674 and lowest-band figures.

The nested date-window totals differ by 466 accounts. Of the 395 additional unblocked accounts, 390 lack the audit's explicit trans marker. This reproduces Mike's 98.7% composition figure. Per-band subtraction cannot uniquely identify earlier-only accounts because a shared account's harshest-post band can change with the date window. We do not describe that subtraction as an identified 314-account historical cohort or as proof of Simpson's paradox.

We independently ran the four classifier strings Mike supplied at a fixed `unitary/unbiased-toxic-roberta` revision. Three rounded scores reproduce. The fourth is 0.000628 in our run rather than his reported 0.00191. All four are below 0.02. The [model record](../analysis/data/toxicity_example_verification.json) includes the actual scores, revision, software versions and weights hash. This verifies scores for the supplied strings; original source-account identity and anonymous sample membership remain unverified.

Two previously mapped role-based harm posts were retrieved again. Neither contains Aaron or Roderick, and both predate the authors' third-bulk blocks. Their text hashes match the earlier capture. This confirms the name-search blind spot. It does not establish their membership in the original unpublished no-contact account list.

## Corrections and unresolved claims

The chart contains 178 no-contact cases across three bulk imports: 15 + 72 + 91. The third import alone contains 91. We correct the thread's description of all 178 as third-import cases.

The thread's written addendum swaps the pronoun and quote bit labels. Its reported pronoun-only counts agree with the retained decoder using bit 8 for pronouns and bit 16 for quote status. The raw chart object is preserved unchanged.

The claimed two identity-label errors in a 25-profile check were not independently verified against the original manual-check records. Neither were full membership of the 178-account cohort, the amount of differential post-recovery loss, or the share of blocks explained by the May and September disputes. These are not presented as measured society findings.

## The audit author's clarification

In a [primary-source follow-up](https://bsky.app/profile/aidap.bsky.social/post/3mxrulsvkqk2u), the audit's author says her opener made the result sound like it came from the bot and separates the live-only result. A [second post](https://bsky.app/profile/aidap.bsky.social/post/3mxrulsvkql2u) identifies the generic harshness measure as the weaker part and discusses the disputed context of camp references. We verified both public records.

The strengthened conclusion remains specific: the audit's behavior comparison does not establish equal relevant conduct and therefore does not establish anti-trans discrimination. The added checks do not estimate a complete campaign-adjusted identity effect.
