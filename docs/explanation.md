# What the methodological problem means

By Friendly Society of Corvids.

## 1. What our review found

The audit omitted documented relevant posts from its comparison of behavior. It therefore does not establish anti-trans discrimination.

Its question was whether blocking patterns differed between account groups after accounting for conduct. Answering that requires measuring the conduct relevant to selection.

## 2. The context of the blocks

Aaron Rodericks and his Trust & Safety team were the subjects of repeated sexual accusations and abusive posts. Public records document this dispute before the September 2026 block imports.

Some posts were addressed to Aaron. Others appeared as standalone posts or in conversations with other users. Aaron's description of the initial selection says it used an AI query of sitewide posts.

## 3. The audit measured only part of that conduct

The behavior score covered replies and quotes directed at Aaron. The initial selection considered posts from across Bluesky.

This is the mismatch: the blocking process considered a broader set of posts than the score used to compare behavior.

## 4. Why the mismatch matters

Consider this hypothetical illustration: two accounts write equally civil replies to Aaron. One also posts a threat in another thread. A score based only on their replies to Aaron could treat their behavior as equivalent, while the broader selection process has additional evidence about one of them.

The hypothetical explains the mechanism. The recovered posts establish that relevant off-scope conduct actually existed.

## 5. What we verified

We matched 13 of Aaron's 50 published examples to exact source posts. Ten appeared before their authors' blocks. Eight of those ten were outside the replies and quotes directed at Aaron.

Those eight posts contain harm-related language that the narrow behavior comparison omitted. The [source table](../analysis/data/verified_sample_sources.csv) links each example to its original post. The [fuller mapping](../mapping/account_post_map.csv) supplies stable post/account identifiers and timing information.

The eight examples establish a concrete omission. They do not measure how widespread that omission was across the full sample.

## 6. Most blocks came from initial lists

Of the 1,271 recovered blocks in the study window, 1,087 (85.5%) were imported in bulk. These initial lists dominate the combined result. Initial selection and later incoming-interaction blocks need separate analysis.

## 7. What the finding establishes

The numerical checks reproduce the audit author's group comparison within the audit's sample. The identity labels have not been independently validated, and the behavior score omitted relevant posts, so this sample comparison does not establish anti-trans discrimination.

Its adjustment for behavior is incomplete. Equal scores on the measured replies and quotes do not establish equal conduct across the posts relevant to initial selection. The audit therefore does not establish anti-trans discrimination.

The amount of the gap explained by the broader conduct remains unmeasured. Answering that requires the original account mapping and comparable pre-decision histories for blocked and unblocked accounts.

## 8. Independently checked additions

Michael Paulauski (@mike10010100.com), who is not a member of Friendly Society of Corvids, identified additional checks in his public thread. We reproduced the subgroup counts and date-window composition, checked the corrected live-only figures, and ran the named model on his classifier strings. We also re-retrieved role-based harm posts that a name-only search misses.

The [independent-analysis notes](independent-analysis.md) credit his contribution and distinguish reproduced findings from corrected or unresolved claims. These checks strengthen the finding that the behavior comparison is incomplete; they do not calculate a full campaign-adjusted identity effect.
